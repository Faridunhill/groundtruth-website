Why this matters

The most expensive surprises come after you own it.

New Jersey and Tennessee both carry land rules that decide a parcel's real value — and they aren't on Zillow. Your realtor and your home inspector don't answer these questions.

"Can I even build?"

Wetlands buffers, overlay districts and steep-slope rules can quietly make a lot unbuildable — or cut its usable area in half.

"Will it flood?"

FEMA flood zone and elevation change insurance cost, financing, and what you're allowed to put on the site.

"Will the soil pass?"

Septic feasibility and soil class decide whether a rural parcel can support a home at all without public sewer.

"What's it really cost?"

A perc test runs $450–$2,300 and a wetland delineation can pass $5,000. Know which parcels deserve that money first.

Questions

Straight answers.

Why is it free? What's the catch?

There isn't one. We're new, and a report nobody has read is worth nothing. A hundred real reports on real parcels is how we find our gaps and learn what buyers actually need — which is also how we'll set a fair price later. You keep the report either way, and nobody in the beta is charged retroactively.

Will you ask for a card?

No. There is no card field, no account, and no trial that converts into a subscription. When the 100 free reports are gone, the beta simply closes.

Is this a home inspection?

No. A home inspector evaluates the physical condition of a structure on site. GroundTruth analyzes the public record around a parcel — flood, wetlands, regulatory overlays, zoning, soils — to tell you what the land will and won't allow. The two are complementary.

How is this different from Zillow or Redfin?

Those are listing databases. GroundTruth produces a verdict: it synthesizes regulatory and environmental data into a clear read on what you can actually do with a parcel — the part a listing never shows.

Does this replace a perc test or a wetland delineation?

No, and it isn't meant to. Those are field studies costing hundreds to thousands of dollars. This report tells you whether a parcel is worth spending that money on — and which questions to ask when you do.

Do I need a specific property?

An address or parcel ID gives the most precise report. If you're still researching an area, say so in the form and we'll scope what we can.

How long does delivery take?

Within two business days. We'd rather set a window we beat every time than promise an hour and miss it. If the queue backs up, you'll get an email with a real date instead of silence.

Is this professional or legal advice?

No. Reports compile public and licensed data for informational purposes and present a suitability verdict for your own decision. They are not legal, financial, real-estate, or engineering advice. Always verify findings independently before acting.
